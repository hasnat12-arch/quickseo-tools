📖 SEO Glossary

Plain-English definitions for the SEO terms you keep seeing but nobody explains simply.

A – B
Alt Text
A short written description attached to an image, read by screen readers and search engines since they can't "see" pictures. Also shown if the image fails to load.
Backlink
A link from another website pointing to yours. Search engines treat backlinks as a vote of confidence — more (quality) backlinks generally means more trust.
Bounce Rate
The percentage of visitors who leave a page without interacting further (clicking a link, scrolling, visiting another page). A high bounce rate isn't always bad — it depends on what the page was supposed to do.
C – D
Canonical URL
A tag that tells search engines which version of a page is the "main" one, used when the same content is reachable from more than one URL, to avoid duplicate-content confusion.
Crawling
The process where a search engine's automated bot visits pages on the web and follows links to discover new ones — the first step before a page can be indexed.
Domain Authority
A third-party score (not an official Google metric) estimating how likely a website is to rank, based mostly on the quantity and quality of backlinks it has.
I – K
Indexing
After crawling a page, a search engine may add it to its index — a giant database of pages it can show in search results. A page can be crawled but not indexed if it's low quality or blocked.
Internal Link
A link from one page on your site to another page on the same site. Helps visitors (and search engines) find related content and understand your site's structure.
Keyword Density
The percentage of times a specific word or phrase appears in a piece of text relative to the total word count. Useful for spotting overuse ("keyword stuffing"), not a strict target to hit.
M – O
Meta Description
A short summary (ideally under ~155 characters) shown below a page's title in search results. Doesn't directly affect ranking but strongly affects whether people click.
Meta Title
The clickable headline shown in search results (ideally under ~60 characters before it gets cut off). Usually the same as, or close to, a page's <title> tag.
Organic Traffic
Visitors who arrive at your site through unpaid search results, as opposed to paid ads or social media links.
R – S
Readability
How easy a piece of text is to read and understand, usually estimated from sentence length and word complexity. Simpler writing tends to keep readers on the page longer.
Robots.txt
A small text file at the root of a website that tells search engine crawlers which parts of the site they're allowed (or not allowed) to crawl.
Schema Markup
Structured data added to a page's code (often as JSON-LD) that explicitly tells search engines what kind of content a page contains — an article, a product, an organization, and so on.
Sitemap
An XML file listing all the important pages on a website, submitted to help search engines discover and crawl content more efficiently.
Slug
The part of a URL that identifies a specific page in readable form, e.g. the best-free-seo-tools in example.com/best-free-seo-tools.
T – U
Title Tag
The HTML element (<title>) that defines a page's title — shown in browser tabs, bookmarks, and usually as the clickable headline in search results.
UGC (User-Generated Content)
Content created by visitors rather than the site owner — comments, reviews, forum posts. Can add fresh, unique content to a page, but usually needs moderation.
